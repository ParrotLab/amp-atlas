# Why we work this way — the AMP operating model

AMP Atlas isn't just a place to store documents. It's built around a specific way of
working — one that keeps your team's knowledge **shared, current, and trustworthy** as
your business grows and as Claude takes on more of the work.

This guide explains the thinking behind it. You don't need it to get started, but once
you understand *why* Systems are shaped the way they are, everything else clicks into
place.

---

## The problem we're solving

Most teams' knowledge is scattered. A process lives in someone's head. The "real"
version of a document is in a Google Doc — or is it the Notion page? The answer to a
question got typed into Slack six months ago and is now unfindable. When someone leaves,
their knowledge leaves with them.

This is already painful for people. It's *worse* when you want Claude to help, because
Claude can only be as good as the context it's given. If your knowledge is scattered,
outdated, or contradictory, Claude works from the wrong information — and you can't even
tell that's what happened.

So the goal is a single, organized, trustworthy home for what your team knows — one that
**both people and Claude** can read from and contribute to, and that stays current
through everyday work rather than a special "let's update the wiki" effort that never
happens.

---

## The three ideas that make it work

### 1. One canonical source per part of your business

Each **System** is the single source of truth for one area — your Learning System, your
Delivery System, your Marketing System. Not three competing versions across three tools.
One home. When something needs to be true for your team, it lives in that System's
**Live Version**, and everyone — human or AI — reads from the same place.

> No more "the AI sees one thing and I see another." You and Claude look at the exact
> same documents.

### 2. Nothing becomes official without a review

The Live Version is trustworthy for one reason: **every change is reviewed before it
lands there.** You make changes in a **Draft**, a teammate looks them over, and only
then does it become official.

This is what stops a knowledge base from quietly rotting. In most tools, anyone can
change anything at any time, and no one notices when something important drifts out of
date. In AMP Atlas, a real person signs off on every change. That single habit is what
keeps the whole System reliable over months and years.

### 3. Structure you can count on

Every System is organized the same way, so you always know where to find things — and so
does Claude. Approved, trustworthy material lives in one place; in-progress drafts and
project work live in another. (The next guide,
[the AMP folder structure](./02-folder-structure.md), walks through exactly how.)

That consistent shape is a **promise**: *if you're looking for X, X is always in the
same place.* It's what lets you (or Claude) walk into a System you've never opened before
and immediately know your way around.

---

## Why not just use a shared drive or a wiki?

Shared drives and wikis are great at storing documents, but they're missing the two
things that make knowledge *trustworthy* over time:

- **Version history and review.** In a shared drive, changes just happen. There's no
  "wait, let me check that before it goes live," and no easy way to see what changed or
  undo it. AMP Atlas gives you a reviewed, recorded history of every change — the same
  discipline serious software teams use to keep big, important systems reliable.
- **A format people and AI both read well.** Your knowledge is stored as plain, readable
  documents — no proprietary format, nothing locked in a tool. That's exactly the kind
  of material Claude reads and reasons about best.

The remarkable part: you get all of that power **without any of the technical
machinery.** AMP Atlas handles the version history, the reviews, and the structure
quietly underneath. You just write, review, and publish.

---

## Where this is heading

Today, the rhythm is mostly: people plan and write the work, Claude helps execute pieces
of it, and people review everything before it's published. Over time, as trust builds,
Claude takes on more — but the shape stays the same. **Every change, whether it came from
a person or from Claude, goes through the same review before it becomes official.** That's
what keeps the system accountable no matter how much of the work AI eventually does.
