# Coworking with Claude on a new project

One of the best things about AMP Atlas is that you don't have to do the work alone. You can
have **Claude** help you draft a whole project — the early thinking, the first versions of
documents — and then review and refine it in AMP Atlas.

This guide explains how that partnership works.

---

## The big idea: Claude and AMP Atlas share the same files

Because your team's knowledge lives in Systems as plain documents on your computer, **Claude
can read and write the exact same files that AMP Atlas shows you.** You work with Claude in
its own app (outside AMP Atlas), and because you're both pointed at the same System folder,
whatever Claude writes shows up in AMP Atlas automatically.

> Think of AMP Atlas as your **live window** into the System. Claude does the work in the
> folder; AMP Atlas is where you *see* and *shape* that work. (There's no Claude built into
> AMP Atlas itself — you run Claude alongside it. A built-in version may come later.)

That arrangement has two big benefits:

- **Claude's work fits your world.** It's drafting from *your* System, so what it produces
  matches how your team actually does things.
- **You can always check its work.** Since it worked from documents you can see, there's no
  mystery about what it based things on.

---

## Keep Claude's work in a Draft

The safe way to cowork is to have Claude's work land in a **Draft** rather than on the Live
Version. Set up (or switch to) a Draft for the project, and let Claude work there. That way
its changes stay private and safe until they've been reviewed — exactly like your own edits.

Nothing Claude produces becomes official until it goes through the normal **review** and
gets **published**. So coworking with Claude doesn't skip any of the safety — it just means
the *first draft* can come from Claude instead of from a blank page.

---

## Two ways to work with Claude

You can partner with Claude in whichever tool you're comfortable with. Both run **outside**
AMP Atlas, and both end up in the same place — files in your System that AMP Atlas shows you.

### Option A — Claude in the chat app
If you like working in a chat, use the **Claude app** (on your desktop or the web), pointed
at your System's folder. You describe what you want, go back and forth, and Claude builds
out the project's documents.

### Option B — Claude Code
If you're more hands-on, **Claude Code** works directly alongside your System's files. It's
a bit more technical, but powerful for larger pieces of work.

> **Not sure which to use?** Start with the **Claude app** — it's the friendlier entry
> point. You can always try Claude Code later. Your team lead can point you to whichever is
> set up for your team, and help with the one-time setup that points Claude at your System.

---

## The everyday flow

Here's what coworking on a new project actually looks like, start to finish:

1. **Set up a Draft** for the project in AMP Atlas, so Claude's work stays safe.
2. **Kick it off with Claude.** Describe the project — the problem, what you want, any
   constraints. Claude can help capture the early thinking and shape the work. (These often
   become a project's *braindump* and *pitch* — see the
   [Templates guide](../04-reference/templates-guide.md).)
3. **Let Claude draft.** Claude builds out the documents in your System's folder.
4. **See it in AMP Atlas.** Open your Draft and read what Claude produced in the clean
   editor — it appears there automatically.
5. **Refine together.** Edit directly, or go back to Claude and ask for changes. Bounce back
   and forth as much as you like — AMP Atlas shows the latest version each time.
6. **When it's ready, submit for review.** Once you're happy, take it through the normal
   [review](./submit-for-review.md) — because Claude's work goes through the same sign-off as
   anyone's.

---

## Your job in the partnership

Claude is fast and capable, but **you're the one with judgment.** Claude can draft a
solution; it can't always tell whether that solution truly fits your team's real needs and
constraints. That's your part. Read what it produced, catch what's off, and shape it until
it's right. The review step exists for exactly this reason.
