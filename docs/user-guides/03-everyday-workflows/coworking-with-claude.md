# Coworking with Claude on a new project

One of the best things about AMP Atlas is that you don't have to do the work alone. You can
have **Claude** help you draft a whole project — the early thinking, the plan, the first
versions of documents — and then you review and refine it in AMP Atlas.

This guide explains how that partnership works.

---

## The big idea: you and Claude share the same System

Because your team's knowledge lives in Systems as plain documents, **Claude can read the
exact same files you can.** When you ask Claude to help with a project, it works from your
actual reference material — your rubrics, your guides, your past projects — not some
separate, invisible copy.

That has two big benefits:

- **Claude's work fits your world.** It's drafting from *your* System, so what it produces
  matches how your team actually does things.
- **You can always check its work.** Since it worked from documents you can see, there's no
  mystery about what it based things on.

---

## Claude always works in a Draft

Just like you, **Claude never changes the Live Version directly.** When Claude helps on a
project, its work lands in a **Draft** — the same safe, private working copy you'd use
yourself. Nothing it produces becomes official until it goes through the normal
**review** and gets **published**.

So coworking with Claude doesn't skip any of the safety. It just means the *first draft* of
the work can come from Claude instead of from a blank page.

---

## Two ways to work with Claude

You can partner with Claude in whichever tool you're comfortable with. Both end up in the
same place — a Draft in your System that you open in AMP Atlas.

### Option A — Claude in the chat app
If you like working in a chat, you can use the **Claude app** (on your desktop or the web),
connected to your System. You describe what you want, go back and forth, and Claude builds
out the project's documents in your System.

### Option B — Claude Code
If you're more hands-on, **Claude Code** works directly alongside your System's files. It's
a bit more technical, but it's powerful for larger pieces of work.

> **Not sure which to use?** Start with the **Claude app** — it's the friendlier entry
> point. You can always try Claude Code later. Your team lead can point you to whichever is
> set up for your team.

Either way, the mechanics of *how* Claude connects to your System are a one-time setup your
team handles — once it's connected, you just talk to Claude about the work.

---

## The everyday flow

Here's what coworking on a new project actually looks like, start to finish:

1. **Kick it off with Claude.** Describe the project — the problem, what you want, any
   constraints. Claude can help you capture the early thinking and shape a plan. (These
   often become a project's *braindump*, *pitch*, and *plan* — see the
   [Templates guide](../04-reference/templates-guide.md).)
2. **Let Claude draft.** Claude builds out the documents in a **Draft** in your System.
3. **Open it in AMP Atlas.** Come into AMP Atlas, open the Draft, and read what Claude
   produced in the clean editor.
4. **Refine together.** Edit directly, or go back to Claude and ask for changes. You can
   bounce back and forth as much as you like — AMP Atlas shows the latest version each time.
5. **When it's ready, submit for review.** Once you're happy, you take it through the normal
   [review](./submit-for-review.md) — because Claude's work goes through the same sign-off as
   anyone's.

---

## Your job in the partnership

Claude is fast and capable, but **you're the one with judgment.** Claude can draft a
solution; it can't always tell whether that solution truly fits your team's real needs and
constraints. That's your part. Read what it produced, catch what's off, and shape it until
it's right. The review step exists for exactly this reason.

---

always lands in a Draft, the two ways to work with it, and the back-and-forth rhythm of
