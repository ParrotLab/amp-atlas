# Submitting your work for review

When your Draft is ready, the last steps are a **review** (a teammate checks your work and
approves it) and then **publishing** (you make it official). This guide walks through both,
and how to ask for the right kind of feedback.

Here's the whole path at a glance:

```
   work in a Draft  ──▶  Submit for review  ──▶  a teammate Approves  ──▶  you Publish  ──▶  Live Version
```

---

## Before you submit

A couple of quick checks:

- **Save your work** so your Draft is up to date (**⌘S**).
- **Give it a once-over.** Read your changes as if you were the reviewer. Is this the change
  you meant to make?

---

## Submitting your Draft

When you're ready, use **Submit for review** — it's one of the options on the **Save** button
in the status bar. A window opens where you:

- See a **summary of what you're publishing** — how many saves and files changed, plus a list
  of your recent change notes, so you and your reviewer can see the shape of the work. (This
  is a summary of your changes, not a line-by-line diff.)
- Give it a **title** and, optionally, a short **description** of what changed and why.
- **Pick your reviewer(s)** — the teammate(s) you want to look it over.

Once you submit, your Draft lands in your reviewer's **Inbox**, under **Needs your review**.

> **Note:** submitting doesn't make your work official. Your Draft stays a Draft — nothing
> changes for the rest of the team — until it's approved and you publish it.

---

## Ask for the right kind of feedback

A quick habit that makes reviews much more useful: in your description (or a quick message),
tell your reviewer **what kind of feedback you actually want.** A helpful way your team
thinks about this is in three levels:

- **Structure** — *Is this organized the right way?* Is the overall approach right? Are the
  right things included, and is anything in the wrong place or missing?
- **Substance** — *Is the content correct and complete?* Are the facts right? Do the details
  hold up? Are there gaps?
- **Style** — *Is this polished and ready to ship?* Does the wording sound right? Is it clean
  and easy to read?

**The order matters:** settle Structure before Substance, and Substance before Style. There's
no point polishing wording on a section whose whole approach might still change.

So match your ask to where the work is: a rough first pass wants a **Structure** review
("is this even the right approach?"); a near-final one wants a **Style** review. Most work
sits in the middle. A one-line note like *"Structure's set — I mainly want eyes on whether
the details are accurate"* saves everyone time.

> **This is a team habit, not a button in the app.** AMP Atlas doesn't have "layer" settings
> — you just say what you want in your description or a message. It's a shared way of talking
> about feedback so reviews land in the right place.

---

## What happens next

Your reviewer will do one of two things (covered from their side in
[Reviewing someone else's work](./review-someones-work.md)):

- **Approve** — your work is good to become official. Approving is a **sign-off; it does not
  publish yet.** Your Draft then shows up in *your* **Inbox** under **Ready to publish**.
- **Request changes** — there's something to address first. They'll leave a **note** saying
  what. You'll see it as reviewer feedback on your Draft.

### If changes were requested
Just keep working in the **same Draft** — make the fixes, save, and submit again. Nothing
starts over. Your reviewer takes another look.

### If it was approved — publish it
Go to your **Inbox → Ready to publish**, and **Publish**. That's the final step: your Draft
becomes the new **Live Version**, and everyone on the team sees it. (Publishing is deliberate
and done by you, the author — approval clears the way, but publishing is the moment it goes
live.)

> **Heads up:** while your Draft is in review, the Live Version might move forward (if someone
> else's work gets published). AMP Atlas brings your Draft up to date automatically when you
> publish. In the rare case your changes clash with theirs, it'll pause and guide you — see
> [When the Live Version changes](./updates-and-conflicts.md).

---

**You should now be able to** submit a Draft for review, tell your reviewer what kind of
feedback you want, handle requested changes without starting over, and publish once approved.
