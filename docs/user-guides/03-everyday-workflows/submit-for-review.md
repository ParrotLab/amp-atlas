# Submitting your work for review

When your Draft is ready, the last step before it becomes official is a **review** — a
teammate reads your changes and either approves them or suggests tweaks. This guide walks
through submitting, and how to ask for the *right kind* of feedback.

---

## Before you submit

A couple of quick checks:

- **Save your work** so your Draft is up to date.
- **Give it a once-over.** Read your changes as if you were the reviewer. Is this the
  change you meant to make?
- **Set the status** on any documents you changed (e.g. from _draft_ to _needs review_),
  so your reviewer knows where things stand.

---

## Submitting your Draft

When you're ready, choose to **publish** your Draft — this starts the review. AMP Atlas
shows you a **summary of your changes** compared to the current Live Version, so you (and
your reviewer) can see exactly what's different.

You'll also **pick your reviewer(s)** — the teammate(s) you want to look it over. Once you
submit, your Draft lands in *their* [Inbox](./review-someones-work.md), and they'll get to
it.

> **Note:** submitting for review doesn't make your work official yet. It stays a Draft
> until it's approved and published. Nothing changes for the rest of the team in the
> meantime.

---

## Ask for the right kind of feedback: the 3 Layers of Work

Here's a habit that makes reviews dramatically more useful. Every piece of work can be
looked at on three levels, and it helps to tell your reviewer **which level you actually
want feedback on.**

- **Structure** — *Is this organized the right way?* Is the overall approach right? Are the
  right things included, and is anything in the wrong place or missing?
- **Substance** — *Is the content correct and complete?* Are the facts right? Do the details
  hold up? Are there gaps?
- **Style** — *Is this polished and ready to ship?* Does the wording sound right? Is the
  formatting clean and easy to read?

**The order matters.** Structure comes before Substance, and Substance comes before Style.
There's no point polishing the wording (Style) of a section whose whole approach might still
change (Structure). Settle the big things first.

### How to use this

Tell your reviewer where you are, so they focus in the right place:

- **Early, rough draft?** Ask for a **Structure** review — "Is this even the right
  approach?" Don't waste anyone's time on typos yet.
- **Approach is settled, checking the details?** Ask for a **Substance** review — "Are the
  facts and specifics right?"
- **Nearly final?** Ask for a **Style** review — "Help me make this polished."

Most work sits in the middle: the structure is settled, but the substance still needs a
careful check. A one-line note like *"Structure's set — I mainly want eyes on whether the
details are accurate"* tells your reviewer exactly what you need and saves everyone time.

---

## What happens next

Your reviewer will either:

- **Approve** your Draft — meaning it's good to become official. Once approved, it can be
  **published** to the Live Version.
- **Request changes** — meaning there's something to address first. They'll leave comments
  explaining what.

If changes are requested, you simply keep working in the **same Draft** — make the fixes,
save, and the review picks up your updates automatically. No need to start over. When your
reviewer is satisfied and approves, you publish, and your work becomes the new Live Version
for everyone.

> **Heads up:** while your Draft is in review, the Live Version might change (if someone
> else's work gets published). If that happens, AMP Atlas will let you know updates are
> available — see [When the Live Version changes](./updates-and-conflicts.md).

---

**layer** of feedback you want, and handle requested changes without starting over. Next,
