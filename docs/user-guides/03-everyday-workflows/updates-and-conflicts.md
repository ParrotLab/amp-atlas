# When the Live Version changes

While you're working away in your Draft, the **Live Version** might change underneath you —
because someone else finished their work and published it. This is completely normal on an
active team, and AMP Atlas is built to handle it gently. This guide explains what happens
and what (if anything) you need to do.

**The short version: nothing you're working on is ever lost, and most of the time you don't
have to do anything at all.**

---

## Why the Live Version moves

Remember that the Live Version is the shared, official version everyone works from. When a
teammate (or Claude's work) gets a Draft approved and **published**, the Live Version updates
for everyone — including you, mid-Draft.

That's a good thing: it means the team's shared knowledge is staying current. It just means
your Draft, which started from an *earlier* Live Version, is now a little behind.

---

## Most of the time, it just works out

Here's the reassuring part: you usually don't have to manage this at all. When you go to
**publish** your Draft, AMP Atlas quietly brings it up to date with the latest Live Version
first, then publishes. If your changes and the new changes are in different places — which is
the normal case — they simply combine, and you'd never even notice.

If you're looking at the **Live Version** itself and want to make sure you're seeing the very
latest, there's a **Refresh** button in the status bar to pull in recent changes. That's the
one manual "get me current" action, and it's only on the Live Version — you don't need to
babysit your Drafts.

---

## When your changes and theirs clash

Occasionally, your Draft changed **the same spot** that someone else's just-published change
also touched. AMP Atlas can't safely guess which version should win, so instead of quietly
picking one, it **pauses and tells you** — clearly and calmly.

When this happens (it shows up when you try to publish), you'll see a plain-language notice
that:

- the **Live Version changed** while you were working,
- lists **which files** are affected, and
- explains how to finish.

Your Draft is safe and unchanged. Here's the simplest way through it:

1. **Refresh the Live Version** (the button in the status bar) so you're looking at the latest.
2. **Start a fresh Draft** from that up-to-date Live Version.
3. **Re-apply your changes** on top — now they sit cleanly alongside the other person's edit,
   with no clash.

Because a clash only happens on the *same lines*, this is also your chance to see the other
change and reconcile the two on purpose, rather than one silently overwriting the other.

> **Comfortable with GitHub?** You can also open the System on GitHub (the **View on GitHub**
> link) and resolve the overlap directly in GitHub's web editor. Either path works — pick
> whichever you prefer.

---

## Nothing is ever lost

The most important reassurance: **your work is safe through all of this.**

- Your Draft is yours. Bringing it up to date at publish time doesn't throw away what you
  wrote.
- If there's a clash, AMP Atlas **stops and asks** rather than silently overwriting anything.
  Your Draft is left exactly as you had it while you get help.
- Publishing only ever goes through once things are safely combined.

So if you see the "the Live Version changed" notice, there's no need to worry. It's just AMP
Atlas being careful on your behalf — and a quick Refresh and re-apply gets you moving again.

---

**You should now understand** why the Live Version changes while you work, that AMP Atlas
catches your Draft up automatically when you publish, that **Refresh** is how you pull the
latest on the Live Version, what the clash notice means and how to bring your Draft up to
date, and — most importantly — that your work is never lost.
